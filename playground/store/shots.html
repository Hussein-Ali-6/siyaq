The bakery that turned the buyout down

By Lena Hart · 5 min read

After a short meeting, Ruth Marlow turned the offer down. A national chain had offered to buy Marlow’s, the family bakery on Fenn Street, and most of the town assumed the deal was done.

Neighbours were not surprised. The bakery has opened at six every morning for forty-one years, through two recessions and a flood, and its sourdough still sells out before nine.

Her reasons were simple. The chain wanted to cut corners on ingredients, and she was not willing to throw decades of goodwill out of the window for a quick payday. “We would rather stay small and keep our name,” she said.

Other shop owners on the street called the decision brave. “Every decision in small business is a trade-off,” said the owner of the hardware shop next door. “She chose the harder one.”

The chain, which has bought eleven independent bakeries in the past three years, said it remained interested in the area and would look at other options.

For now, the queue outside Marlow’s starts at a quarter to six, as it always has.
